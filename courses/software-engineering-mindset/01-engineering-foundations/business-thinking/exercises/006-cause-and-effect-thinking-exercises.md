# Exercise 006 — Cause-and-Effect Thinking

## Objective

Practice tracing business problems through their consequences and investigating possible causes without jumping to conclusions.

The goal is to develop the habit of asking:

```text
What happened?
      ↓
What happened because of it?
      ↓
What caused it?
      ↓
What evidence supports that cause?
      ↓
What else could explain it?
```

---

# Core Principle

> Do not stop at the first obvious explanation.

A visible problem is often only one part of a larger chain.

Instead of:

```text
Problem
  ↓
Assumed Cause
  ↓
Solution
```

Think:

```text
Problem
  ↓
Effects
  ↓
Possible Causes
  ↓
Evidence
  ↓
Confirmed Understanding
  ↓
Business Need
```

# Mastery Check

You are ready to move forward when you can consistently:

- Distinguish problems from causes.
- Distinguish causes from effects.
- Trace consequences forward.
- Trace possible causes backward.
- Generate multiple possible causes.
- Avoid accepting a stakeholder's explanation as proven fact.
- Identify evidence needed to test a hypothesis.
- Ask useful cause-investigation questions.
- Separate facts, hypotheses, and investigations.
- Recognize that one problem may have multiple contributing causes.
- Explain why root-cause understanding matters before designing software.
