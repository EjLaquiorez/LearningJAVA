# Exercise Objective

Practice identifying what matters most to stakeholders and recognizing trade-offs between competing business goals.

Do not jump directly to software solutions.

For each exercise, think in this order:

```text
Stakeholder
    ↓
Goals
    ↓
Priority
    ↓
Constraints
    ↓
Trade-off
    ↓
Evidence / clarification
```

---

# Exercise 001 — Identify the Priority

### Scenario

A restaurant owner says:

> "I want customers to receive their food within 10 minutes, but I also don't want to increase our kitchen staff because labor costs are already high."

### Questions

1. What is the owner's priority?
2. What constraint does the owner have?
3. What trade-off exists?

### Goal

Distinguish the desired outcome from the business constraint and identify the competing interests.

---

# Exercise 002 — Priority vs. Request

### Scenario

A pharmacy manager says:

> "We need a notification system that immediately tells customers when their prescriptions are ready. We cannot afford many additional staff calls."

### Questions

1. What is the request?
2. What is the possible priority?
3. What constraint is mentioned?
4. Why should we avoid assuming the notification system is automatically the correct solution?

### Goal

Separate the requested solution from the business priority behind it.

---

# Exercise 003 — Competing Stakeholder Priorities

### Scenario

A university wants to improve its enrollment process.

The registrar says:

> "Students need to complete enrollment faster."

The finance department says:

> "We need additional payment verification before enrollment is finalized."

### Questions

1. What is the registrar's priority?
2. What is the finance department's priority?
3. Where is the trade-off?
4. What might happen if we optimize entirely for speed?
5. What might happen if we optimize entirely for verification?

### Goal

Recognize that two stakeholders can have legitimate but competing priorities.

---

# Exercise 004 — Limited Resources

### Scenario

A small software company wants to improve its customer support.

The owner wants:

- Faster responses
- 24/7 support
- Lower operating costs
- More support staff

But the company has a limited budget.

### Questions

1. Which goals compete with one another?
2. What resource is limited?
3. Why can't we automatically treat every goal as the highest priority?
4. What questions would you ask the owner before proposing a solution?

### Goal

Understand how resource constraints affect business priorities.

---

# Exercise 005 — Investigate the Trade-off

### Scenario

A restaurant manager says:

> "We need more kitchen staff during lunch because orders are delayed."

The owner responds:

> "Adding staff will increase labor costs. We need to keep the current staffing level."

### Questions

1. What is the manager's priority?
2. What is the owner's priority?
3. What is the trade-off?
4. What evidence would you collect before deciding whether more staff are actually necessary?
5. What alternative explanations could exist for the delays?

### Goal

Avoid accepting a proposed solution before understanding the actual business problem.

---

# Exercise 006 — Priority Ranking

### Scenario

A hospital department has four goals:

- Reduce patient waiting time
- Reduce operating costs
- Improve patient safety
- Reduce staff workload

The department cannot maximize all four at the same time.

### Questions

1. Which goal would you expect to require special consideration before the others?
2. Why?
3. Which goals might conflict?
4. What information would you need before ranking the goals?
5. Who should help determine the priorities?

### Goal

Understand that engineers should not invent business priorities without understanding stakeholder needs and constraints.

---

# Exercise 007 — Hidden Trade-off

### Scenario

A delivery company says:

> "We want every package delivered as quickly as possible."

The operations manager says:

> "But we cannot increase delivery costs."

### Questions

1. What is the desired outcome?
2. What is the constraint?
3. What is the trade-off?
4. What evidence would help determine an acceptable balance?
5. What question would you ask the business before designing software?

### Goal

Identify the trade-off between service quality and cost.

---

# Exercise 008 — Stakeholder Conflict

### Scenario

A school principal wants teachers to spend less time completing administrative forms.

The school administrator says detailed records are necessary to track student progress.

### Questions

1. What is the principal's priority?
2. What is the administrator's priority?
3. What resource or constraint is creating the trade-off?
4. What information would help determine which records are actually necessary?
5. What should an engineer avoid assuming?

### Goal

Connect stakeholder priorities with the conflict-analysis skill from Topic 014.

---

# Exercise 009 — Evidence Before Optimization

### Scenario

An online store owner says:

> "We need a faster website because customers are abandoning their carts."

### Questions

1. What is the owner's stated priority?
2. What assumption is being made?
3. What evidence would you collect?
4. How would you determine whether website speed is actually causing abandonment?
5. What other causes might explain abandoned carts?

### Goal

Learn that a priority does not automatically prove its assumed cause.

---

# Exercise 010 — Full Stakeholder Trade-off Analysis

### Scenario

A restaurant owner says:

> "Customers complain that lunch orders take too long. I want a mobile ordering system because it should make ordering faster. However, the kitchen manager says the real problem is food preparation capacity. I don't want to hire more staff because labor costs are already high."

### Questions

Analyze the situation:

1. Who are the stakeholders?
2. What is the owner's request?
3. What is the owner's priority?
4. What is the kitchen manager's concern?
5. What is the owner's constraint?
6. What trade-off exists?
7. What assumptions are being made?
8. What evidence would you investigate?
9. What questions would you ask before deciding whether a mobile ordering system is appropriate?
10. What would you avoid deciding without evidence?

### Goal

Combine all Topic 015 skills into one realistic business situation.

# Final Validation Exercise

## Scenario

A pharmacy has received complaints that customers wait too long for prescriptions.

The pharmacy owner says:

> "We need an automated notification system so customers know when their prescriptions are ready."

The pharmacy manager says:

> "The bigger problem is that prescriptions are taking too long to prepare."

The owner adds:

> "I want the process to be faster, but I don't want to hire more staff."

### Your Task

Analyze the situation independently.

Identify:

1. Stakeholders
2. Requests
3. Goals
4. Priorities
5. Concerns
6. Constraints
7. Trade-offs
8. Assumptions
9. Evidence to investigate
10. Questions that should be asked before proposing a solution

### Mastery Requirement

You should be able to explain:

> **What does each stakeholder value, what limits the business, where do the goals compete, and what evidence is needed before deciding what should be prioritized?**
