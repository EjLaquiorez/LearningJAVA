# Exercise 010 — Bottleneck Thinking

## Objective

Practice identifying bottlenecks in business processes.

Focus on:

- Where work accumulates
- Where people wait
- Which activity limits the process
- The effect of a bottleneck
- Possible alternative explanations
- Questions that require investigation

---

## Engineering Principle

Do not assume something is slow means it is the bottleneck.

Instead:

1. Observe the process
2. Find where work accumulates
3. Identify the restricted flow
4. Investigate
5. Understand the bottleneck

---

## Skills Practiced

- Bottleneck identification
- Evidence-based reasoning
- Process observation
- Alternative explanation generation
- Investigative questioning

---

# Practice Scenarios

## Exercise 1 — Bakery

A bakery receives customer orders throughout the morning.

Process:

```text
Customer
    ↓
Cashier takes order
    ↓
Order goes to Kitchen
    ↓
Kitchen prepares food
    ↓
Food is packed
    ↓
Customer receives order
```

The bakery reports:

> "Orders are taking too long during the morning rush."

You observe:

- Several customers are waiting.
- The cashier usually finishes orders quickly.
- Orders appear to accumulate before the kitchen.
- The kitchen has two workers.
- Packing usually happens quickly once food is ready.
- Customers receive their orders shortly after packing.

Identify:

- The likely bottleneck
- Evidence supporting your conclusion
- What happens because of the bottleneck
- One alternative explanation

---

## Exercise 2 — Supermarket

A supermarket has the following checkout process:

```text
Customer
    ↓
Choose Checkout
    ↓
Cashier scans items
    ↓
Payment
    ↓
Receipt
    ↓
Customer leaves
```

During busy periods:

- Customers form a long line.
- Cashiers scan items continuously.
- Payment usually happens quickly.
- Customers leave shortly after payment.

Identify:

- Where the process appears to be restricted
- What evidence supports your conclusion
- Where work accumulates
- What information you would investigate before concluding that the cashier is the bottleneck

---

## Exercise 3 — Car Repair Shop

A repair shop follows this process:

```text
Customer
    ↓
Service advisor
    ↓
Vehicle inspection
    ↓
Repair
    ↓
Quality check
    ↓
Customer pickup
```

Customers complain that vehicles are often completed later than expected.

You discover:

- Service advisors process customer requests quickly.
- Several vehicles are waiting for inspection.
- Repairs usually begin shortly after inspection.
- Quality checks are normally completed quickly.

Identify:

- The likely bottleneck
- The evidence
- The effect on the rest of the process
- One question you would ask before deciding that inspection is the actual bottleneck

---

## Exercise 4 — Bottleneck vs Slow Activity

Consider:

```text
Activity A
    ↓
Activity B
    ↓
Activity C
    ↓
Activity D
```

Activity B takes longer than the other activities.

Answer:

1. Does a slower activity automatically mean it is the bottleneck?
2. What additional evidence would you look for?
3. Where would you look for signs of a bottleneck?

---

## Exercise 5 — Investigation Before Solution

A restaurant says:

> "Customers are waiting too long for their food."

The process is:

```text
Customer
    ↓
Waiter takes order
    ↓
Kitchen receives order
    ↓
Kitchen prepares food
    ↓
Food is served
```

Write five investigation questions that could help determine where the bottleneck actually is.

Do not suggest solutions.
Do not mention:

- Software
- Automation
- Databases
- APIs
- Technology

Focus only on understanding the process.

---

## Reflection

After completing the exercises, answer:

1. What is a bottleneck?
2. How is a bottleneck different from a slow activity?
3. What evidence can indicate that work is accumulating?
4. Why should engineers investigate before deciding what the bottleneck is?
5. Why is a bottleneck not necessarily the root cause?
