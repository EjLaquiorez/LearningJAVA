# Glossary

> Important terms learned in Module 1 – Engineering Foundations

---

## Actor

A person, organization, or external system that interacts with or performs actions within a business process or software system.

**Examples:**

- Customer
- Cashier
- Bank Customer
- ATM System
- Payment Gateway

---

## Stakeholder

A person, group, or organization that is affected by or has an interest in the system.

**Examples:**

- Business Owner
- Customer
- Employee
- Manager
- Software Development Team

---

## Workflow

The sequence of activities performed to complete a business process from start to finish.

**Example:**

Customer Places Order

↓

Cashier Accepts Order

↓

Payment Received

↓

Order Prepared

↓

Customer Receives Product

---

## Business Process

A collection of related activities that work together to achieve a business goal.

**Example:**

Ordering bread from a bakery.

---

## Problem

The actual issue or pain point that needs to be solved.

A problem is **not** the requested solution.

**Example:**

❌ "We need a mobile app."

✅ Customers cannot easily track their orders.

---

## Solution

A method used to solve a problem.

Software is only one possible solution.

---

## Root Cause

The underlying reason a problem exists.

Finding the root cause prevents solving only the symptoms.

---

## Requirement

A need or condition that the system must satisfy.

Requirements are discovered by asking questions and understanding the business.

---

## Input

The information or resources provided to a system before processing begins.

**Examples:**

- Customer Order
- PIN
- Product Quantity
- Payment Amount

---

## Process

The operations performed on the input to produce the desired output.

**Examples:**

- Validate PIN
- Calculate Total
- Check Inventory
- Update Database

---

## Output

The result produced after the process is completed.

**Examples:**

- Receipt
- Cash
- Updated Balance
- Sales Report

---

## IPO (Input-Process-Output)

A simple model for analyzing how a system works.

```
Input
   ↓
Process
   ↓
Output
```

Almost every software system can be understood using this model.

---

## Module

A self-contained part of a larger software system that has a specific responsibility.

**ATM Example:**

- Authentication Module
- Withdrawal Module
- Deposit Module
- Receipt Module

---

## System

A collection of components working together to achieve a common goal.

A software system is made up of many smaller modules.

---

## Systems Thinking

Understanding that changes in one part of a system can affect many other parts.

Example:

Changing inventory logic may also affect:

- Sales
- Reports
- Customer Orders
- Accounting

---

## Business Value

The measurable benefit software provides to a business.

Examples include:

- Saving time
- Reducing costs
- Increasing sales
- Improving customer experience

---

## Constraint

A limitation or rule that affects how a solution can be designed.

Examples:

- Budget
- Time
- Technology
- Business Rules
- Legal Requirements

---

## Edge Case

An uncommon or exceptional situation that the system must handle correctly.

ATM Examples:

- Wrong PIN
- ATM Out of Cash
- Network Failure
- Cancelled Transaction

---

## Analysis

The process of understanding a problem before designing a solution.

Analysis includes:

- Asking questions
- Observing workflows
- Identifying stakeholders
- Finding root causes

---

## Engineering Mindset

A way of thinking that prioritizes understanding problems before implementing solutions.

Engineers focus on:

- Asking questions
- Logical reasoning
- Breaking problems into smaller parts
- Designing before coding

---

## Software Engineering

The discipline of applying engineering principles to design, build, test, deploy, and maintain software systems that solve real-world problems.

---

## Key Principle

> "Code is the implementation of a well-understood solution to a well-defined problem."
