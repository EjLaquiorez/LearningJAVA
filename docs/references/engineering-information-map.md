# Engineering Information Map

## Purpose

The Engineering Information Map is a mental framework used to understand a business before proposing solutions.

Whenever a client describes a problem, analyze these ten areas before thinking about software.

---

# 1. Process

## Question

How does the work happen today?

## Goal

Understand the sequence of activities performed by the business.

---

# 2. People

## Question

Who is involved?

## Goal

Identify everyone participating in the business process.

---

# 3. Data

## Question

What information moves through the process?

## Examples

- Names
- Orders
- Payments
- Inventory
- Dates
- Status
- Reports

Software is built around data.

---

# 4. Pain

## Question

What is the business problem?

Examples:

- Long waiting time
- Wrong orders
- Lost inventory
- Late deliveries

---

# 5. Cause

## Question

Why does the problem happen?

Never assume.

Investigate the root cause.

---

# 6. Frequency

## Question

How often does the problem occur?

Examples:

- Every transaction
- Daily
- During peak hours
- Occasionally

Frequency helps determine priority.

---

# 7. Impact

## Question

Who is affected?

Possible impacts:

- Customers
- Employees
- Managers
- Revenue
- Reputation

---

# 8. Constraints

## Question

What limits possible solutions?

Examples:

- Budget
- Time
- Regulations
- Employee skills
- Existing technology
- Internet availability

---

# 9. Goal

## Question

What does success look like?

Examples:

- Faster service
- Fewer mistakes
- Lower costs
- Higher customer satisfaction

---

# 10. Risks

## Question

What could go wrong?

Examples:

- Employee resistance
- Poor adoption
- Increased costs
- Security issues
- Technical failures

---

# Engineering Principle

Before proposing any solution, understand all ten areas.

Missing information leads to poor engineering decisions.
